Percentages show up in prices, wages, grades and statistics. Most mistakes come from using the wrong base: the number the percentage is "of". Here are the formulas, each with an example you can check in the [Percentage Calculator](/tools/percentage-calculator/).

## Percentage of a number

**Formula:** percentage ÷ 100 × number

What is 15% of 240? 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = **36**.

## One number as a percentage of another

**Formula:** part ÷ whole × 100

You answered 42 of 50 questions correctly: 42 ÷ 50 × 100 = **84%**.

## Percentage increase or decrease (percentage change)

**Formula:** (new − old) ÷ old × 100

Rent rises from 800 to 860: (860 − 800) ÷ 800 × 100 = **7.5% increase**. A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

If the old value is zero, the percentage change is undefined, because you cannot divide by zero.

## Percentage difference

When neither value is the "original", use the average as the base.

**Formula:** |a − b| ÷ ((a + b) ÷ 2) × 100

Two shops charge 40 and 50: 10 ÷ 45 × 100 = **22.2% difference**. Unlike percentage change, swapping the two numbers gives the same answer.

## Adding or subtracting a percentage

Multiply by (1 + p/100) to increase, or (1 − p/100) to decrease. A price of 60 with 8% tax: 60 × 1.08 = 64.80.

## Common mistakes

### Reversing a percentage change

A 50% fall followed by a 50% rise does not return to the start. 100 → 50 → 75. The second percentage applies to a smaller number.

### Adding percentages together

A 20% discount followed by a further 10% discount is 28% off in total, not 30%: 100 × 0.8 × 0.9 = 72. Try it with the [Discount Calculator](/tools/discount-calculator/).

### Confusing percentage points with percent

If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it rose by **one percentage point**, which is a **25% increase** in the rate.

### Using the wrong base

"Our price is 20% lower than theirs" and "their price is 20% higher than ours" are not the same statement, as the base differs. Check which number you divide by.

## FAQ

### How do I convert a decimal to a percentage?

Multiply by 100. 0.375 becomes 37.5%.

### How do I find the original number before a percentage increase?

Divide the new value by (1 + p/100). If a price is 66 after a 10% rise, the original was 66 ÷ 1.10 = 60.

### Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. A value that doubles has increased by 100%; a value that triples has increased by 200%.
