Printing a contract just to sign it and scan it back is slow. You can place your signature on the PDF itself instead. This guide explains the options, how to get a clean result, and where the limits are.

## Which kind of "signature" is it?

The word covers several different things:

- **A signature image.** A picture of your handwritten, drawn or typed signature, placed on the page. This is what the [PDF Signer](/tools/sign-pdf/) adds.
- **An electronic signature service.** A platform that records who signed, when, and keeps an audit trail.
- **A digital (certificate-based) signature.** A cryptographic signature tied to a certificate, which can show whether the document changed after signing.

An image signature is fine for many everyday forms, but it proves little by itself: anyone could paste the same picture. Whether it is acceptable depends on the document, the recipient and the law where you live. If a contract, a government form or a financial agreement requires a specific kind of signature, ask the recipient what they accept.

## Step by step

1. **Open the PDF** in the [PDF Signer](/tools/sign-pdf/). It stays in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
2. **Create your signature.** Draw it with a mouse, finger or stylus, or type your name in a handwriting-style font.
3. **Drag it to the signature line.** Use the corner handle to resize it so it sits on the line without covering printed text.
4. **Add a date.** Choose "Add date or text", pick a format that matches the document, and place it beside the signature.
5. **Check every page** that needs a mark, then download the signed PDF.

### Tips for a signature that looks right

- Make the signature large enough to be legible, but smaller than the signature box.
- Draw slowly with a thicker pen on a phone; thin lines look faint on a small screen.
- Zoom in after placing it: check that no line of printed text is covered.
- Use navy or black. Bright colours can look like a stamp rather than ink.

## Using a photo of your real signature

If you prefer your genuine handwriting:

1. Sign with a dark pen on plain white paper in good light.
2. Take a straight-on photo with no shadows.
3. In the signer, choose "Add image" and keep "Remove background" ticked. Check the preview on the chequered background. Transparent areas show the pattern.

Crop tightly first if the photo has a lot of surroundings, using the [Image Cropper](/tools/image-cropper/).

## Initials on every page

Some agreements ask for initials on each page. Insert your initials once, place them in the footer, and use "Copy to all pages". Check one of the later pages to confirm the position suits it, since pages can have different layouts.

## Before you send it

- **Keep the original.** The signed copy has the signature built into the pages, so it cannot be moved afterwards.
- **Check the file size.** If it is too big to email, see [how to reduce PDF size](/blog/reduce-pdf-size-for-email/).
- **Combine supporting pages.** Attach an ID scan or schedule by [merging PDFs](/blog/how-to-merge-pdf-files/) into one file, if the recipient wants a single document.
- **Protect your signature.** Don't post a signed document publicly, since a clean copy of your signature can be copied from it.

## What this does not do

It does not fill in form fields, add a certificate, or prove the document was not altered afterwards. The original text and images stay as they were, so selectable text remains selectable.

## FAQ

### Is a drawn signature legally valid?

In many places electronic signatures can be valid, but requirements differ by country and document type. Some need a qualified electronic signature or a handwritten original. Ask the recipient or a legal professional if it matters.

### Does the tool store my signature?

Only if you choose "Save this signature in this browser". It is kept in your browser's local storage on that device, not uploaded, and you can delete it from the "Saved signatures" window.

### Can I sign on a phone?

Yes. The signature pad accepts touch and stylus input, and you can drag and resize items with your fingers.
