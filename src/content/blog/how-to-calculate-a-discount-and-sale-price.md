A sign says "30% off". What will you actually pay? This guide shows the arithmetic so you can check any sale yourself, and the [Discount Calculator](/tools/discount-calculator/) does it instantly.

## The basic formula

**Sale price = original price × (1 − discount ÷ 100)**

A jacket costs 80.00 and is 25% off:

- 1 − 25 ÷ 100 = 0.75
- 80.00 × 0.75 = **60.00**
- You save 80.00 − 60.00 = **20.00**

A shortcut: 10% of any price is that price with the decimal point moved one place. Take 10% of 80 (8.00), double it for 20%, and add half of 10% (4.00) to get 25%, which is 20.00 off.

## Finding the discount from two prices

Sometimes you only see the old and new price. The discount percentage is:

**(old − new) ÷ old × 100**

A game falls from 50 to 35: (50 − 35) ÷ 50 × 100 = **30% off**. The [Percentage Calculator](/tools/percentage-calculator/) has a card for this, and our guide on [percentage increase and decrease](/blog/percentage-increase-decrease-guide/) explains the idea.

## Stacked discounts: why 20% + 10% is not 30%

When a store gives 20% off and then a further 10% coupon, the second discount applies to the **already reduced price**:

- 100.00 × 0.80 = 80.00
- 80.00 × 0.90 = **72.00**

You saved 28.00, which is 28%, not 30%. Two percentages never add up directly unless they apply to the same starting number.

## Where sales tax or VAT fits

In most places the tax is calculated on the price you actually pay. The [Discount Calculator](/tools/discount-calculator/) adds tax **after** the discounts:

- Discounted price 72.00
- Tax at 8%: 72.00 × 0.08 = 5.76
- Total: **77.76**

Rules differ by country, product and retailer. Some stores treat coupons as payment instead of a price reduction, which can change the tax. If it matters, check your receipt.

## Quick sanity checks

- A bigger percentage off a higher price is not always a better deal. Compare the final prices, not the labels.
- "Up to 50% off" only guarantees that the best item has 50% off.
- Watch for "before" prices that were never charged. The calculator cannot tell you whether the original price was fair.

## FAQ

### How do I calculate the original price from the sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). If something is 60.00 after 25% off, the original was 60.00 ÷ 0.75 = 80.00.

### Does the order of two discounts matter?

No. 20% then 10% gives the same total as 10% then 20%, because the multipliers 0.80 and 0.90 multiply in any order.

### Does the currency change the result?

No. The currency option in the calculator only changes how the numbers are displayed. It does not convert between currencies.
